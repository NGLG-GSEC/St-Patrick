The Story

A younger Order with an old symbolic language

The Order draws from the legacy of Irish knighthood, Saint Patrick, and the Masonic custom of preserving select workings through qualified brethren. Its public record shows a modern structure centred on a Grand Lodge, a Grand Commander, warranted Lodges and honours granted for service.

The Order is distinct from the historic British chivalric Order of Saint Patrick. The Masonic Order uses the same Irish spiritual vocabulary as inspiration while operating as a separate Masonic body.

Foundational memory John Raymond Shute II is listed among the Grand Commanders and Grand Cross recipients.

Identity & Eligibility

The Order, AMD and Mark Masons' Hall

This section is written to remove the most common confusion: the Masonic Order of Saint Patrick is a distinct invitational Masonic Order. It is not the Allied Masonic Degrees itself, and it is not administered by Mark Masons' Hall. Its relationship with those bodies is best understood through qualification, shared appendant-Masonic culture and the Red Branch of Eri.

01

What the Order is

The Masonic Order of Saint Patrick is an invitational Order with its own Grand Lodge, Grand Commander, officers, honours and warranted Lodges. Its symbolism is Irish and Christian in tone, using Saint Patrick, the shamrock, the cross, the lorica tradition and the motto Quis Separabit as public markers of identity.

02

What it is not

It should not be confused with the historic Most Illustrious Order of Saint Patrick founded in 1783 as a British order of chivalry. Nor should it be presented as a degree of the Allied Masonic Degrees. It is a separate Masonic body with its own roll, charters and administration.

03

Relationship with AMD

The connection with AMD is one of qualification and Masonic pathway, not identity. Public membership wording for the Order refers to the Red Branch of Eri under an AMD or SRIA constitution as one accepted qualifying route. In other words, AMD may supply a qualifying background for some Knights; it does not make the Order an AMD council.

04

Mark Masons' Hall

Mark Masons' Hall in London is the administrative home of several companion Orders in England and Wales, including the Allied Masonic Degrees. Therefore the link is indirect: where a Brother's qualifying AMD membership is under the English and Welsh system, that AMD administration is connected with Mark Masons' Hall. The Masonic Order of Saint Patrick remains separately governed.

Who May Be Invited

Admission is by invitation and by qualification

A candidate should be a subscribing Master Mason in a regular and recognised Craft Lodge, a subscribing Royal Arch Mason in a regular and recognised Royal Arch Chapter or equivalent body, and a subscribing member of a recognised Masonic chivalric or allied body accepted by the Order.

  • Regular Craft standing Membership in a regular and recognised Craft Lodge.
  • Royal Arch standing Membership in a regular and recognised Royal Arch Chapter, or accepted equivalent.
  • Knight Masonry and Red Branch of Eri Knight Masonry (Red Cross of Babylon in certain jurisdictions) is required; the Red Branch of Eri under an AMD or SRIA constitution is preferred.
  • Continuing membership Qualification is not only for admission; membership in the qualifying bodies should be maintained.

Education & Foreign Relations

The Order, the Allied Masonic Degrees and the path between them

A plain guide for inquiring brethren: what the Order is for, where it came from, what is required to be considered, and how it stands in relation to the Allied Masonic Degrees (AMD) and Mark Masons' Hall.

01

Purpose

The Order recognises Freemasons distinguished by vision, leadership and devoted service. It draws on the mission of Saint Patrick, revered as the Patron Saint of Ireland and admired for his devoted service to God and humanity. Its themes are service, charity, Psalm 23 and moral courage, with the shamrock as the unity of the Triune God.

02

History

The Order grew from the research of John Raymond Shute II, the “Incomparable Innovator” of Monroe, North Carolina, who was first Sovereign Grand Master of the Grand Council of the Allied Masonic Degrees of the USA in 1932. His ritual was rediscovered by Sir Knight David Lee Hargett, Jr. in 2005 and reactivated on 1 February 2006. It is not connected to the British Order of Saint Patrick of 1783.

03

Requirements

Admission is strictly by invitation. Obligatory: Ancient Free and Accepted Masonry (Craft), the Holy Royal Arch, and Knight Masonry (the Red Cross of Babylon in certain jurisdictions). Preferred: the Red Branch of Eri, under the Allied Masonic Degrees or the Societas Rosicruciana in Anglia. Originally the Shute System also looked for Cryptic and Knight Templar membership, with preference for those who had held office.

Foreign Relations

How the Order stands with AMD and Mark Masons' Hall

The relationship is one of invitation and qualification. AMD councils supply brethren; the Order is conferred only on those invited from among them.

  • AMD of France The Grand Conseil des Grades Maconniques Allies en France, under its Sovereign Grand Master, received a Charter on 20 April 2022 to form Lodges of the Order within its jurisdiction and admit duly qualified brethren. In King Athelstan Council No. 926 the Order is listed among the Invitational Orders for Red Branch members.
  • AMD of India Also chartered in 2022 to work the Degree under its own Constitution.
  • Mark Masons' Hall The London home of several companion Orders in England and Wales, including AMD. The link is indirect, through a Brother's qualifying AMD membership. It does not govern the Order.
  • Governance Charters, honours, officers and admission remain with the Grand Commander and Grand Lodge of the Order.

The Path to the Order

From the Craft Lodge to the Order of Saint Patrick

The Masonic Order of Saint Patrick is a distinct, invitational Masonic Order with its own government, its own offices, its own Constitutions, its own Lodges and its own symbolic tradition. It is not a degree of the Craft, nor of the Royal Arch, nor a Council of the Allied Masonic Degrees. It stands as a separate Order, yet it asks of every candidate a particular Masonic pathway and a fitting Masonic standing.

The pathway begins with the regular Masonic foundation. The candidate must first be a subscribing Master Mason, a member of a regular and recognised Craft Lodge. This is the cornerstone. He must then be a Companion of the Holy Royal Arch, a member of a regular Chapter, or of the equivalent recognised body where another jurisdiction so provides. Royal Arch membership shows that the Brother has advanced beyond the bounds of the Craft and gained a wider understanding of the Masonic tradition.

Beyond these, the Order looks for a further connection with a recognised Masonic chivalric or allied body. Here the relationship with the Allied Masonic Degrees appears. The Order of Saint Patrick is not AMD, but AMD may be one of the roads that shows a Brother's fitting Masonic preparation. Knight Masonry is required, which in certain jurisdictions is the Red Cross of Babylon, and membership of the Red Branch of Eri, under an AMD or SRIA Constitution, is the preferred qualification.

Put simply, AMD does not govern the Order of Saint Patrick, and the Order of Saint Patrick is not a degree of AMD. Their relationship is one of qualification, kinship and Masonic pathway. A Brother who has worked in AMD or in a related recognised body may have the right foundation to be invited into the Order, provided he also meets the remaining requirements.

The Order carries a chivalric and Christian-Irish symbolism. The name of Saint Patrick, the shamrock, the cross, the thought of spiritual protection and the motto Quis Separabit speak of unity, fidelity, fraternal devotion and constancy. Entry is not a simple enrolment but an invitation into a particular Masonic family with a defined spiritual and symbolic identity.

In brief, a Brother is suitable for invitation when he:

  1. Is a subscribing Master Mason in a regular and recognised Craft Lodge.
  2. Is a subscribing Companion of a Royal Arch Chapter, or of an equivalent recognised body.
  3. Is a Knight Mason (Knight Masonry, which in certain jurisdictions is the Red Cross of Babylon), and preferably also a member of the Red Branch of Eri under an AMD or SRIA Constitution.
  4. Maintains active membership and good Masonic standing in the bodies from which he comes.
  5. Is invited by the Order, since entry is not gained by petition alone but by invitation and acceptance by the competent officers.

Mark Masons’ Hall is linked to the matter only indirectly. In England and Wales it is the administrative home of several companion Orders, among them the Allied Masonic Degrees. That does not mean Mark Masons’ Hall governs the Masonic Order of Saint Patrick. The connection is administrative only as regards AMD and the other bodies housed or governed there. The Order of Saint Patrick remains a separate Order with its own administrative structure.

The plainest account is therefore this: a Brother begins in the Craft Lodge, continues in the Royal Arch, gains further Masonic experience in a recognised allied or chivalric body, and, if judged suitable, may be invited into the Masonic Order of Saint Patrick. The Order replaces none of these bodies; it stands upon that pathway as a particular Masonic honour, duty and fraternal fellowship.

    Read the nine degrees in full, with their jewels
    I

    St Lawrence the Martyr

    Must be taken first. It explains how Lawrence gave his life rather than betray his principles; responsibilities, integrity and fortitude are the lessons to be learned here.

    The Jewel Gridiron in silver suspended from an orange ribbon with royal blue edging.

    II

    Knight of Constantinople

    Concerns the relationship between the Emperor Constantine and his Subjects. The ceremony is entertaining and amusing but at the end, the candidate is left in no doubt of the importance of the virtue of humility.

    The Jewel Maltese cross in gold or gilt suspended from a green ribbon on which are 3 poinards in gold or gilt with the points downwards.

    III

    Grand Tilers of Solomon

    Very dramatic, set in the secret vault beneath the Temple. It reflects on the danger of hasty judgment.

    The Jewel Triangle with the apex pointing downwards in black enamel edged with gold or gilt, inscribed with the Hebrew characters 27 and on the obverse the Ineffable Name in Cabalistic Order, suspended from a fiery red ribbon with a pale grey edging either side of which is a hand holding a dagger with the point downwards and surmounted by three Crowns, all in gold or gilt.

    IV

    Red Cross of Babylon

    Fills the gap between the Craft and the Holy Royal Arch and deals with Zerubbabel obtaining permission to commence the rebuilding of the Temple. The lesson here is of the supreme importance of truth.

    The Jewel Seven pointed star on which are two crossed swords, points upwards, in gold or gilt, on a green enamel circle suspended from a green ribbon.

    V

    Grand High Priest

    Many believe this should be the last of these five Degrees taken. The candidate is anointed, consecrated and set apart to the service of God in the High Priesthood. A truly uplifting ceremony which is rather special.

    The Jewel Mitre superimposed on a triangle, apex pointing upwards, in gold and gilt, suspended from a red ribbon.

    VI

    Architect

    First of a Solomonic trilogy that elaborates on the Master Mason degree, with Continental structure, English floorwork and catechisms on the tools of temple construction and trestle boards.

    The Jewel Flaming star with the letter G within a gold triangle.

    VII

    Grand Architect

    Continues the architectural sequence once attached to the Early Grand Rite of Scotland, for an increasingly select body of craftsmen working on the Solomonic Temple.

    The Jewel Double triangle formed by the Compasses and the Level.

    VIII

    Superintendent

    The climax of the architectural series and the ne plus ultra of Solomonic architecture. It requires extensive preparation and properties.

    The Jewel Gold triangle with a purple central orb.

    IX

    Masters of Tyre

    Originated in North Carolina, USA. It highlights the Tyrian connection to Masonry rather than Jerusalem alone and teaches the paramount duty of the craftsman to his lodge and calling.
