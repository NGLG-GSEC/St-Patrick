The Story

A younger Order with an old symbolic language

The Order draws from the legacy of Irish knighthood, Saint Patrick, and the Masonic custom of preserving select workings through qualified brethren. Its public record shows a modern structure centred on a Grand Lodge, a Grand Commander, warranted Lodges and honours granted for service.

The Order is distinct from the historic British chivalric Order of Saint Patrick. The Masonic Order uses the same Irish spiritual vocabulary as inspiration while operating as a separate Masonic body.

Foundational memory John Raymond Shute II is listed among the Grand Commanders and Grand Cross recipients.

Identity & Eligibility

The Order, AMD and Mark Masons' Hall

This section is written to remove the most common confusion: the Masonic Order of Saint Patrick is a distinct invitational Masonic Order. It is not the Allied Masonic Degrees itself, and it is not administered by Mark Masons' Hall. Its relationship with those bodies is best understood through qualification, shared appendant-Masonic culture and the Red Branch of Eri.

01

What the Order is

The Masonic Order of Saint Patrick is an invitational Order with its own Grand Lodge, Grand Commander, officers, honours and warranted Lodges. Its symbolism is Irish and Christian in tone, using Saint Patrick, the shamrock, the cross, the lorica tradition and the motto Quis Separabit as public markers of identity.

02

What it is not

It should not be confused with the historic Most Illustrious Order of Saint Patrick founded in 1783 as a British order of chivalry. Nor should it be presented as a degree of the Allied Masonic Degrees. It is a separate Masonic body with its own roll, charters and administration.

03

Relationship with AMD

The connection with AMD is one of qualification and Masonic pathway, not identity. Public membership wording for the Order refers to the Red Branch of Eri under an AMD or SRIA constitution as one accepted qualifying route. In other words, AMD may supply a qualifying background for some Knights; it does not make the Order an AMD council.

04

Mark Masons' Hall

Mark Masons' Hall in London is the administrative home of several companion Orders in England and Wales, including the Allied Masonic Degrees. Therefore the link is indirect: where a Brother's qualifying AMD membership is under the English and Welsh system, that AMD administration is connected with Mark Masons' Hall. The Masonic Order of Saint Patrick remains separately governed.

Who May Be Invited

Admission is by invitation and by qualification

A candidate should be a subscribing Master Mason in a regular and recognised Craft Lodge, a subscribing Royal Arch Mason in a regular and recognised Royal Arch Chapter or equivalent body, and a subscribing member of a recognised Masonic chivalric or allied body accepted by the Order.

  • Regular Craft standing Membership in a regular and recognised Craft Lodge.
  • Royal Arch standing Membership in a regular and recognised Royal Arch Chapter, or accepted equivalent.
  • Chivalric or allied qualification Knight Masons, Red Branch of Eri under AMD or SRIA constitution, or accepted equivalent.
  • Continuing membership Qualification is not only for admission; membership in the qualifying bodies should be maintained.

Education & Foreign Relations

What the Order is for, where it came from and how it relates to others

A plain guide for inquiring brethren: the purpose and history of the Order, what is required to be considered, and how it relates to the Allied Masonic Degrees and Mark Masons' Hall.

01

Purpose

The Order exists to bring qualified Masons together in fellowship under a disciplined, chivalric and Christian-toned symbolism drawn from Saint Patrick, the shamrock, the cross and the lorica tradition. Membership is framed as duty, fidelity and service, not simple affiliation, and the Order is open to qualified brethren who are invited.

02

History

The modern Order traces its public record through Lorica Council Time Immemorial (2017), the recognition of Lorica Lodge as Time Immemorial under the Grand Lodge of the Order (2024), and the Charter granted for Limerick Lodge in Kentucky (2026). It operates through a Grand Lodge, a Grand Commander and warranted Lodges, and is separate from the historic British Order of Saint Patrick founded in 1783.

03

Requirements

Admission is by invitation and qualification: a subscribing Master Mason in a regular and recognised Craft Lodge, a subscribing Royal Arch Mason, and a subscribing member of a recognised chivalric or allied body accepted by the Order, such as Knight Masons or the Red Branch of Eri. Membership in those bodies should be maintained.

Foreign Relations

Allied Masonic Degrees and Mark Masons' Hall

  • Allied Masonic Degrees (AMD) A separate body. For some Knights, AMD membership is a qualifying route into the Order through the Red Branch of Eri; the Order is not an AMD council.
  • Mark Masons' Hall The London home of several Orders in England and Wales, including AMD. It is connected to the Order only through a Brother's qualifying AMD membership and does not govern the Order.
  • The Order's own governance Charters, honours, officers and admission remain with the Grand Lodge and the Constitutions of the Order.

The Allied Masonic Degrees Pathway

Five degrees that add to your knowledge of both Mark and Royal Arch

To join the Allied Masonic Degrees, which is one qualifying route to this Order, you must be a Royal Arch Mason as well as a Mark Master Mason; the five degrees worked will add to your knowledge of both. They are open to Brethren who are Mark and Royal Arch Masons and of any faith. The degree of St Lawrence the Martyr must be taken first.

I

St Lawrence the Martyr

Must be taken first. It explains how Lawrence gave his life rather than betray his principles; responsibilities, integrity and fortitude are the lessons to be learned here.

The Jewel Gridiron in silver suspended from an orange ribbon with royal blue edging.

II

Knight of Constantinople

Concerns the relationship between the Emperor Constantine and his Subjects. The ceremony is entertaining and amusing but at the end, the candidate is left in no doubt of the importance of the virtue of humility.

The Jewel Maltese cross in gold or gilt suspended from a green ribbon on which are 3 poinards in gold or gilt with the points downwards.

III

Grand Tilers of Solomon

Very dramatic, set in the secret vault beneath the Temple. It reflects on the danger of hasty judgment.

The Jewel Triangle with the apex pointing downwards in black enamel edged with gold or gilt. Inscribed with the Hebrew characters 27 and on the obverse the Ineffable Name in Cabalistic Order, suspended from a fiery red ribbon with a pale grey edging either side of which is a hand holding a dagger with the point downwards and surmounted by three Crowns, all in gold or gilt.

IV

Red Cross of Babylon

Fills the gap between the Craft and the Holy Royal Arch and deals with Zerubbabel obtaining permission to commence the rebuilding of the Temple. The lesson here is of the supreme importance of truth.

The Jewel Seven pointed star on which are two crossed swords, points upwards, in gold or gilt, on a green enamel circle suspended from a green ribbon.

V

Grand High Priest

Many believe this should be the last Degree taken. The candidate is anointed, consecrated and set apart to the service of God in the High Priesthood. A truly uplifting ceremony which is rather special.

The Jewel Mitre superimposed on a triangle, apex pointing upwards, in gold and gilt, suspended from a red ribbon.

Editorial note: the five degrees above are those of the Allied Masonic Degrees, not degrees of the Masonic Order of Saint Patrick. Details of jewels and ritual should be confirmed with the competent authority of the relevant body.

Meaning

Symbolism with a disciplined centre

The seal and motto

The seal combines the shamrock, cross, gold border and the motto Quis separabit. The visual system suggests protection, fidelity, Christian memory and unity of the brethren.

01

Lorica

A prayer of protection in the Hiberno-Scottish Christian tradition, used here as a moral image of spiritual armour.

02

Chivalry

The Order’s titles and ceremonial language frame membership as duty, fidelity and service rather than simple affiliation.

03

Recognition

Charters speak of properly vouched brethren, mutual recognition and obedience to the Grand Lodge and its successors.

04

Continuity

Time Immemorial status recognises past achievement while preserving a Lodge within the present jurisdiction.
