The Story

A younger Order with an old symbolic language

The Order draws from the legacy of Irish knighthood, Saint Patrick, and the Masonic custom of preserving select workings through qualified brethren. Its public record shows a modern structure centred on a Grand Lodge, a Grand Commander, warranted Lodges and honours granted for service.

The Order is distinct from the historic British chivalric Order of Saint Patrick. The Masonic Order uses the same Irish spiritual vocabulary as inspiration while operating as a separate Masonic body.

Foundational memory John Raymond Shute II is listed among the Grand Commanders and Grand Cross recipients.

Meaning

Symbolism with a disciplined centre

The seal and motto

The seal combines the shamrock, cross, gold border and the motto Quis separabit. The visual system suggests protection, fidelity, Christian memory and unity of the brethren.

01

Lorica

A prayer of protection in the Hiberno-Scottish Christian tradition, used here as a moral image of spiritual armour.

02

Chivalry

The Order’s titles and ceremonial language frame membership as duty, fidelity and service rather than simple affiliation.

03

Recognition

Charters speak of properly vouched brethren, mutual recognition and obedience to the Grand Lodge and its successors.

04

Continuity

Time Immemorial status recognises past achievement while preserving a Lodge within the present jurisdiction.
